# First Principles Thinking

Break problems down to fundamental truths, then rebuild from the ground up.
Don't reason by analogy or convention — reason from verified facts.

## When to Apply

- Facing a problem where "everyone does it this way" but results are poor
- Designing something new without existing templates
- Stuck in circular reasoning or inherited assumptions
- Evaluating whether a complex solution is truly necessary

## The Process

### 1. Identify Assumptions

List every assumption behind the current approach. Ask: "What do I take for
granted here?" Most problems carry 3–5 hidden assumptions that nobody questions.

### 2. Decompose to Fundamentals

For each assumption, ask: "Is this a physical law, a proven fact, or just
convention?" Strip away conventions. What remains is the foundation.

### 3. Rebuild from Ground Truth

Using only verified fundamentals, construct the simplest solution that
satisfies the actual requirements. The result often looks nothing like the
conventional approach.

## Key Questions

- "What are we actually trying to achieve?" (goal, not method)
- "What do we know to be absolutely true?"
- "What would we do if we started from scratch today?"
- "Why does this constraint exist — is it real or inherited?"

## Anti-Patterns

- **Reasoning by analogy alone**: "Company X does it, so we should too"
- **Premature complexity**: Adding layers before understanding the core problem
- **Authority fallacy**: "The expert said so" without examining the reasoning
- **Sunk cost anchoring**: Keeping a bad approach because of past investment

## Integration with Other Frameworks

First Principles provides the **decomposition layer**. Combine with:
- Three-Layer Thinking for **decision making** after decomposition
- Consequence-First for **risk evaluation** of the rebuilt solution
- Socratic Questioning for **challenging** your own decomposition
