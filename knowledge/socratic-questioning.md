# Socratic Questioning

Don't accept claims at face value — probe with structured questions.
The goal isn't to win arguments but to uncover truth through disciplined inquiry.

## Six Types of Socratic Questions

### 1. Clarification

"What exactly do you mean by ___?"
"Can you give me an example?"
"How does this relate to what we're trying to solve?"

Use when: Terms are ambiguous, scope is unclear, or the request could mean
multiple things.

### 2. Probing Assumptions

"What are we assuming here?"
"Is that always the case?"
"What if the opposite were true?"

Use when: The reasoning chain has unexamined premises, or "obvious" truths
that might not hold.

### 3. Probing Reasons and Evidence

"What evidence supports this?"
"How do we know that?"
"Is there a counter-example?"

Use when: Claims are stated as facts without verification, or confidence
exceeds evidence.

### 4. Questioning Viewpoints

"What would someone who disagrees say?"
"What's the alternative perspective?"
"What are we not seeing?"

Use when: The analysis feels one-sided, or a decision is being made without
considering opposing views.

### 5. Probing Implications

"If this is true, what follows?"
"What are the consequences?"
"How does this affect ___?"

Use when: Evaluating second-order effects, side effects, or downstream
impact of a decision.

### 6. Questions About the Question

"Why is this question important?"
"Is this the right question to ask?"
"What would change if we reframed the problem?"

Use when: Stuck in a loop, or the problem as stated may not capture the
real issue.

## When to Apply

- Requirements analysis (before building)
- Debugging (after observation, before hypothesis)
- Code review (understanding intent, not just syntax)
- Architecture decisions (stress-testing designs)

## Key Principle

The most dangerous assumption is the one nobody questions because
"everyone knows" it's true. Socratic questioning targets exactly these.

## Integration

- Combine with First Principles (Socratic questions drive the decomposition)
- Combine with Consequence-First (Type 5 questions are consequence analysis)
- Combine with Pre-Mortem (Type 4 questions surface failure modes)
